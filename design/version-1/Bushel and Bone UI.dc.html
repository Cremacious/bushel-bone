Iconography

Single-weight 1.6px stroked line, open ends, no fills, no rounded cartoon corners: an engraver's outline, not an app icon. Drawn on a 30px grid at 1.5x for desktop. Weather and resources only. Never a glyph where a word will do.

The Dusk reveal. Day-book lines write on one at a time, 160ms apart, as if entered by hand. The omen block, if there is one, arrives last and alone.
Weather
A slow particle wash over the location plate only, never over type. Rain streaks, snow drifts, dry air holds a heat shimmer. Under 6 percent opacity of movement; it should be noticed second, not first.

Omen
Dread never animates fast. A stain breathes on a 5.5s cycle. A crow at the plate's edge blinks once a minute. As Reckoning rises the cycle shortens and the stain widens. No shake, no flash, no red.
Reduced motion

Turn becomes a 120ms opacity fade. Tick, bleed and rule become instant state changes. Weather and omen loops stop entirely and hold their mid-cycle frame, so the stain is still visible, just still.

A third setting

Settings carries Motion: Full, Reduced, None, defaulted from the system preference rather than overriding it. None also stops the omen loop, for players who find the breathing stain unpleasant.

Never animated

Body prose never types on, and a scene title never writes itself. The player reads at their own pace; making them wait for words is the one motion sin this game cannot afford.

What changed, and why

The place is a banner, not a panel. Where you are reads across the top of the screen under the masthead, so it is answered before anything else and costs no column. The speaker stands in the corner of the room with their nameplate fixed to their own frame, which is where a nameplate belongs.

The leaf floats, and it is wide. One object, 1004px, holding the ledger, the household line and the reading column, with the seasonal spine on its binding edge. It is the only thing the player reads, so it is the only thing that looks like paper.

The roster is a button, not a panel. The household collapses to one line inside the leaf, names and morale and regard, with Show roster at its right end. Nothing about the labor force needs its own floating card on the desk.

The day is legible. "Day 1 of 20" plus five season pips, which the old build never showed. A player could not tell where they were in a season.

Six tabs, named. Home, Fields, Hands, Town, Ledger, Almanac. Home is the current beat; Almanac is the codex and the help. On mobile the beat's choices are always reachable above the tab bar, and every row clears 56px.

Dread at this setting: a vignette that is a touch too dark for a spring morning, and rain that falls on the illustration and not on the page. Nothing else. The chrome will get worse as Reckoning climbs.

Notes

Choice cards keep the dual label the clarity pass added, but the mechanical tag moves to Courier Prime at 16px and is colored by valence, so a player scanning four options reads the costs first without reading the prose twice.

Unaffordable choices state the arithmetic. "You have 61 marks. This wants 80." No hover, no info glyph, no 40 percent opacity guessing game.

Warnings live under the ledger, not inside the four figures. Two can fire at once and neither truncates. That was the old strip's failure case.

The omen is a footer, not a banner. It sits below the choices, in omen violet, on its own rule, and it never carries a number. The crow on the illustration is doing the same work in a different register.

The plate is the room. The illustration is the whole 1920 canvas rather than a 440px box in a column, so weather and omens have somewhere to happen and the speaker can stand 580px tall instead of 154px.

Motion: choosing plays Bleed on the card, then Turn to the result. The omen block fades in 400ms after the rest of the leaf has settled, so it arrives as an afterthought.

Notes

The plate became the field map. Four large parcels fill the left of the screen, each with its own furrow direction, and each one shows the decision being made on the right: a Just set chip the moment a crop is chosen, a growth bar on what is already in the ground, and the Stone Lot drawn grey and stony with its fertility circles empty. Setting a crop changes the land, not just a list.

The running spend moved into the ledger bar. It used to be a 13px italic note under the fields, which is the one number a player checks constantly.

A spent field says so in words. The old build showed three empty circles and an info glyph. Here the row carries the consequence in a full sentence and the Fallow chip is promoted to green with "mends 1".

Mobile does not shrink this, it folds it. Only the field being set is expanded; the others collapse to a one-line summary. Six chips become a 3 × 2 grid at 56px minimum, never a single-file column.

Motion: selecting a crop plays Bleed on the chip and Ticks the spend figure in the ledger bar. No layout shift, because the chip grid is fixed.

Notes

Day mode is not a light-mode afterthought. The desk becomes foxed paper, the leaf becomes brighter paper, and the plate keeps its engraved double border because on light paper a framed illustration needs the frame.

Contrast: ink on leaf is 11.4:1 in day and 12.1:1 in night. The softest text in the system, ink-faint on paper, is 4.7:1, so even the labels clear AA at 14px.

The report gains two lines the old build folded away: what the fires burned, and the weather that caused it. Winter is the beat where fuel becomes the story.

The omen arrives last and alone, 1.1s after the final figure, on its own violet rule. It is a Walkers-tier line here, and it still gets no number, no icon, no sound sting.

Notes

A true modal. Centered on the whole canvas over a scrim that dims the beat behind it, with two ways out: the ✕ at the top right and the Close control at the bottom. The panel never touches an edge, so it reads as something laid on top of the game rather than a drawer sliding in.

Two columns on desktop because a 1180px overlay with one column of hands wastes half the panel and pushes graves below the fold.

The graves get their own section, headed "In the ground." In the old build a dead hand was a span in the same household row as the living, which read as a bug.

Morale dots are 20px with 5px tracking and are colored by band: green in good heart, amber worn, red spent. The condition word stays, because a dot count alone is not a reading.

On mobile it is a tab, not a modal. The roster is consulted every dawn; making it a place with a persistent tab removes the open-close-reopen loop the overlay forced.

How it holds

Many hands. The household becomes a scrolling rail on the left, one 44px row per hand, and the leaf widens to 1360px to take the space back. Above nine hands the rail scrolls and the summary line in its header ("9 housed · 3 graves") is what the player reads at a glance.

Long names. Rows are flex with the name allowed to ellipsis and the morale dots pinned with flex:0 0 auto, so "Thomasina Ashgrove-Pike" truncates rather than pushing the dots off the rail. The full name is always in the roster, where there is room.

Stacked warnings. The warning band is a column, not a row: three fire here and each gets a full line. It grows downward and the reading column shrinks, which is the correct trade when the house is on fire.

Long scene text. Past roughly 600 characters the desktop stage splits into prose and a choice column, so the reading measure never exceeds 74 characters and the choices stay above the fold.

150 percent type. Nothing is clipped, because the phone layout is a flex column with one scrolling region and every other band sized by content. The ledger folds from four cells to a 2 × 2 grid, warnings wrap to two lines, choice rows grow to 64px, and the tab bar drops its icons and keeps its labels.

Dread scales too. At Warnings tier the stain's cycle drops from 5.5s to 3.4s and it grows by half. That is the only thing on screen that tells the player the hidden number moved.

Dropping this back into year1.html

Every block above is one element with border, padding and inline-safe rules. The existing class names survive: .ledger becomes the brass bar, .hand keeps its shape, .btn keeps its dual label, .plate-cap and .plate-nameplate are unchanged in structure. What is not answered here

The Town, Ledger and Almanac tabs are named but not designed, since none of them exists in the prototype yet. The market screen reuses the choice card and stepper wholesale. Festival and Long Vigil beats use the play scene with no changes.

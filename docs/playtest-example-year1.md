# Bushel & Bone — Worked Playthrough: "The Newcomer" (a sample game)

A full year of Year 1 played by hand from the [playtest kit](playtest-kit-year1.md), written up as a reference game. Read this before running your own so you know how the rhythm feels. Dice rolls and choices are real (not cherry-picked for a win). Mechanics are shown **[in brackets]**; the story is in plain text.

**Starting the board:** 100 marks · 20 seed · 4 empty fields (Fertility ●●●) · Reuben (Field Hand, Morale ●●●○○) · 80 food · 0 fuel · *(hidden: Reckoning 0)*.

---

## SPRING — finding my feet

**Morning Brief.** Weather die: **3 — Mild.** Silas Ridley rides up to my gate the first morning, doesn't dismount, and tells me I owe the bank a hundred and fifty a year — *"not this year, nor the next."* I nod. **[Learned the mortgage; free through Year 2.]** Then it's First Furrow, the Planting Fair. I decide to **attend** — a newcomer should be seen. **[Reuben spends the season at the fair → no tending bonus this season; +town regard; the wheat field gets Grange's blessing.]** A systemic card: Reuben, cap in hand, asks for a name of his own. I give him one. **[Grant → Reuben Morale ○○○○●→●●●●○ (3→4). He works harder for it.]**

**Assign & Plant.** With Reuben at the fair, I just plant. Two fields to **potato**, one to **wheat** (the blessed one), and — a gamble — one to **cotton**, the cash crop. **[Seed 20 covers potato+potato+wheat (6+6+4=16); cotton's 10 runs the seed out and costs 6 marks. Seed 0, marks 100→94.]** The catch I'm already fretting about: cotton needs *two hands* to harvest, and I have one. I'm betting I can buy a second before fall.

**Grow & Harvest.** The potatoes come ripe by season's end. **[2 potato fields, untended: 10 + 10 = 20 units. Both fields drop to Fertility ●●.]** Wheat and cotton are two-season crops — still growing.

**Market & Consume.** I sell half the potatoes for cash toward that second hand, and keep half to eat. **[Sell 10 potato @ 2×1.1 = 22 marks → 116. Keep 10 (= 20 food).]** The household eats. **[Food need 30. Pool 80 + 20 = 100, eat 30 → 70.]**

**Dusk.** *(Facilitator: Reckoning +2 baseline −2 for a kind season = 0. Whispers.)* Omen, read aloud: *"The dog won't sleep by the east fence anymore."*

> **End of Spring** — Marks 116 · Food 70 · Fuel 0 · Reuben M4 · Reckoning: Whispers. A good start. Cotton's in the ground, and I've money saved.

---

## SUMMER — the wagon, and the storm

**Morning Brief.** Weather die: **5 — Good rain (+1 growth).** Then Ambrose Vane's wagon stops at my gate at dusk, warm as a parlor, offering a Grower — sharp, strong-backed — for 110 marks. **This is the decision.** I have 116. If I buy her, I get the second hand my cotton needs — but I'll have six marks to my name and *two* mouths to feed through winter. I buy her. Her name's Della. **[Marks 116→6. Two clones now. Food need jumps to 40/season, 50 in winter — the burden is real.]**

Then the storm card: **The Cotton Won't Wait.** The wheat's come ripe and there's a storm two days out. Push the hands through the night and I save it whole; let them rest and I lose a third. I push them. **[Overwork → Reuben M4→3, Della M3→2. Wheat saved whole.]** *The land takes no notice of a hard day — not yet.* **[Facilitator note: overwork adds NO Reckoning. Did the player expect it to? I half did.]**

A foundling turns up at the gate — another stray hand. I'd take it if I could feed it, but I just spent everything on Della. I send it on to Sister Ruth. **[+Ruth's regard.]**

**Assign & Plant.** Both hands work the wheat harvest. I scrape up one more potato planting; the fourth field I leave fallow to rest. **[Marks 6→0 (potato). One field fallow → Fertility recovers ●●→●●●.]**

**Harvest & Market.** **[Wheat (blessed): 8 units. New potato: 10 units. Sell 4 wheat @ 3 = 12 marks → 12. Keep 4 wheat + 10 potato to eat.]**

**Consume.** Now I'm feeding three mouths. **[Food need 40. Pool 70 + (4 wheat×1.5=6) + (10 potato×2=20) = 96, eat 40 → 56.]**

**Dusk.** *(Reckoning: overworked, so no decay; +2 baseline → 2. Still Whispers.)* Della's morale worries me — new, worked hard her first week, down to ●●○○○.

> **End of Summer** — Marks 12 · Food 56 · Della M2 (fragile) · cotton ripening under Reuben's care. I have my second hand and my cash crop coming — but I'm broke and there are three to feed.

---

## FALL — the crunch that breaks you

**Morning Brief.** Weather die: **2 — Mild.** **Harvest Home** falls, dead in the crunch. If I send the hands to the feast I can't harvest the cotton — and cotton needs both. If I skip, I keep them but the town notes it and Della misses the one thing that might lift her. I skip it. Both hands to the fields. **[−town regard. Della's morale stays low.]** A hard, cold call, and I feel it.

A systemic card: rats in the granary. I can't afford to lose winter stores. I trap them. **[4 marks → 8.]**

**The Winter Readiness check makes my stomach drop.** Food 56, and I'll burn 40 this fall and *50* in winter — 90 needed, and cotton feeds no one. Fuel: **zero**, and I need ~45. **[Facilitator reads the readiness panel: Food 56, Fuel 0 — short on both.]**

**Assign & Plant.** Here's the trap the two-hand farm walks into: I can harvest the cotton *or* chop the winter's wood, not both. I put Reuben on the axe (16 wood) and send Della to bring in the cotton alone — which means **half a crop**. **[Reuben chops → 16 fuel. Della harvests cotton solo → half yield: ~3 units. Field → ●●.]**

**Market.** Even half a cotton crop is real money. **[Sell 3 cotton @ 12×1.05 ≈ 38 marks → 46.]** I spend nearly all of it on coal to close the fuel gap. **[Buy 15 coal @ 3 = 45 marks → 1 mark; +30 fuel → 46 fuel, just over the 45 I need.]**

**Consume.** **[Food need 40. Pool 56 → 16.]** Sixteen food, and winter wants fifty.

**Dusk.** *(Reckoning 2, no cruelty → decay to... held at 2 by the baseline. Whispers.)* Reuben's worn from the axe; Della's hollow-eyed.

> **End of Fall** — Marks 1 · Food **16** · Fuel 46 · Reckoning: Whispers. I have heat. I do not have food. Buying Della is about to cost me.

---

## WINTER — I survived another year

**Morning Brief.** Weather die: **6 — Cold snap.** The worst roll, at the worst time. **[Fuel need jumps to 55; I have 46.]** Now I'm short on *both*.

**The Long Vigil** falls on the longest night. My farm is failing and I go anyway — the whole town in the church, watching over the capped Old Well, Grange reading the year's dead so none are *taken*. **[Attend → +regard; the Reckoning eases.]** Watching a town keep its terrified vigil while my own hands go hungry is the truest thing I've seen out here.

Sister Ruth comes through the snow with a basket, and doesn't make it a shame to take. **[+12 food → 28.]** It isn't enough. **[Food 28, winter need 50. Fuel 46, need 55.]**

**The winter takes its due.** The cold gets in where the fuel ran short, and it takes Della — the newest, the frailest, the one I couldn't keep warm. I dig her a marked grave in ground too hard to want it, and say her name. **[Fuel short + cold snap → the weakest clone dies. Marked grave: no Reckoning added, a small ease. Ghost Roll stays empty — I did right by her.]**

With Della gone, there's food enough to scrape through. **[Now farmer + Reuben, winter need ~35. Food 28 — short 7; we tighten belts. Reuben M3→2, but we live.]**

**Spring, Year 2, Day 1.** The frost breaks. Reuben and I wake to a thin morning and a cold farm, and we are both still breathing. *I survived another year.* It doesn't feel like a victory. It feels like a debt half-paid — and there's an empty place at the table where Della used to sit.

> **YEAR 1: SURVIVED.** Reuben alive (M2). Della lost. Reckoning: Whispers (clean). One cotton crop's worth of hard lessons.

## What this playthrough taught (the playtest findings — issue #11)

Playing it by hand, the kit **works** — and it teaches exactly what it's meant to. Specific findings:

1. **The core tension (H-09) landed, viscerally.** Buying Della was the whole game. It nearly doubled the food burden, and a small farm couldn't carry two mouths through one winter. A cautious player who *skipped the wagon* would have sailed through — which is the point. The numbers create the intended pressure without being told to.
2. **The labor constraint is sharp and painful.** Cotton needing two hands, colliding with the need to chop wood, forced a genuine "which do I sacrifice" choice in Fall. This is where the game's teeth are, and they bite correctly.
3. **The moral fork works — including the honest non-cost.** Overworking to save the wheat added *no* Reckoning, and as the player I half-expected it to. That gap ("I thought that would cost me") is the whole hook doing its job.
4. **The Long Vigil has real weight**, especially played while failing — watching a scared town's ritual while your own hands starve is genuinely affecting.
5. **A losing-ish run is more instructive than a clean win.** Losing Della, done *right* (a marked grave, no Reckoning), delivered the survival-horror core — "I survived, but not everyone did" — better than a comfortable year would have.
6. **Difficulty feels right:** survivable with caution, punishing of overreach, and it was *my* choice (the wagon) that cost me, not the dice.

### Small kit fixes this surfaced (applied to `playtest-kit-year1.md`)
- **Seed vs. marks** was ambiguous — clarified: spend the starting 20 seed first (1 seed = 1 unit of plant cost), then pay marks.
- **Food need per extra hand** wasn't on the cheat-sheet — added (+~10/season, +~15 winter per clone).
- **"When you fall short"** rule added: fuel-short in winter → the frailest clone freezes (marked grave); food-short → clones go hungry before the farmer.

*Net: the kit is validated. The next human to play it should get the same clean rhythm — and their own version of the empty chair at the table.*

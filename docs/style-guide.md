# Bushel & Bone — Voice & Style Guide

**Purpose:** lock the prose voice before we write the ~120 event cards (#16), the NPC dialogue (#13), and the arc text (#14/#15). This is the writers' contract. It fulfills Narrative Bible **Part 6** (the voice guide); Part 6 now points here.

**The one-line brief:** *plain, weathered, alt-1800s American-frontier register — King-James cadence without the archaic spelling — where the horror lives in an ordinary detail, never in an adjective.* A crow at the window beats an army of ghosts (CLAUDE.md working conventions).

---

## 1. The register

- **Alt-1800s, readable — not pastiche.** We evoke the period; we do not perform it. No "thee/thou," no dropped-g dialect spelling ("workin'"), no phonetic accents. Diction and rhythm carry the era, spelling stays clean.
- **Weathered and bodily.** Nouns are concrete and rural: soil, frost, pail, rope, bone, tallow, sedge. Verbs do the work: sour, dig, turn out, quicken, mend, bury.
- **Understated.** The narrator does not gasp. It reports. The reader supplies the dread.
- **Second person for the player** ("You buried her unmarked…"), present-leaning framing for event cards. NPCs speak in their own voices (§6).

## 2. Do / Don't

**DO**
- Lead every card with a concrete, sensory image.
- Allow **one** supernatural detail per card, at most. Let it sit unexplained.
- State cruelty and its cost plainly — never with relish, never with a wink.
- Use the em-dash and the period. Let a short declarative land, then occasionally open into one longer rolling sentence.
- Trust the reader to infer the Marrow. We never narrate the ledger.

**DON'T**
- No modern slang, therapy-speak, or game-speak (see §3's forbidden list).
- No exclamation-point spam. (A single one, rarely, from a panicked NPC.)
- No fourth-wall gags, memes, or authorial jokes.
- No adjective piles ("a terrible, ghastly, unnatural fog"). Cut to the noun.
- No gore for its own sake. Adult, not gratuitous.
- Never explain *why* the milk soured. Show that it did.

## 3. Diction — the world's words vs. the forbidden

**Words the game uses (build the world with these):**
- *The trade:* vessel, the grown, husk (cruel), quicken, vesselwright, vat-liquor, the Vat, quick ground.
- *The land & faith:* the Marrow, the seam, the Sull, the Vigil, the Watch, the naming, rite, unmarked, come-back, the turned-out, the Old Well.
- *Frontier & work:* mark (money), iron (a coin), charter, mortgage, homestead, bunkhouse, fallow, bushel, the paper (the almanac/news).
- *Period-safe color:* reckon, yonder (sparingly), a spell (of time), fixing to, kin, plain (as "it's plain that"), sound (as "the cow is sound").

**Words the game NEVER uses (they break the spell):**
- *Modern casual:* okay, hey, yeah, guys, kids (say **children**), stuff, cool, awesome, fine ("I'm fine"), nervous-modern "so…".
- *Therapy/HR register:* trauma, cope, process (feelings), boundaries, toxic, closure, self-care.
- *Game/tech register:* grind, loot, buff, level up, XP, stats, optimize, meta, spawn.
- *Anything winking or contemporary.* If a word could appear in an office e-mail, cut it.

## 4. Rhythm & punctuation

- Favor the **period** and the **em-dash**. Semicolons: rare. A **comma splice** is permitted, occasionally, for a breathless rural cadence — *"She won't say it, she just sets the pail down and goes."*
- Sentence length varies: mostly short, one long roll per paragraph for weight.
- Italics only for a single emphasized word or a *heard* whisper. Never ALL CAPS.
- Numbers in flavor prose are spelled where small ("two hands short"); mechanical values (costs, yields) render as digits in the choice line.

## 5. The five prose principles

1. **Restraint over spectacle.** The smallest wrong detail is the loudest.
2. **Concrete before abstract.** Name the pail, the crow, the warm patch of ground.
3. **The land never speaks; it shows.** Symptoms, not sermons.
4. **Cruelty is plain, not lurid.** We do not enjoy it, and we do not look away.
5. **Ambiguity is load-bearing.** The Church and Old Nan disagree; so should the prose. Never resolve on the page what the design leaves unsettled (D-030).

## 6. NPC voice tics (the 10 — codified from Part 2)

| NPC | Voice | Never |
|---|---|---|
| **Halloway** | expansive, anecdotal, sells the future; never a plain "no" | admits the founding sin aloud |
| **Grange** | scripture-cadence that frays into tired, plain asides | certainty he doesn't feel (it cracks) |
| **Doc Bell** | dry deflection, gallows humor, precise about bodies | sentiment; a straight comfort |
| **Meredith** | quick, teasing, transactional; turns questions into prices | gives anything away free |
| **Silas** | bloodless financial euphemism; fear leaks at omens | plain warmth |
| **Bess** | bright surface, one sharp true question underneath | flattery |
| **Old Nan** | fragments, indirection; the land is "she" | a straight answer (always a true one, never a straight one) |
| **Coldwater** | clipped, plain, no euphemism, moral weight | euphemism; small talk |
| **Ambrose** | cultured eastern polish; makes the monstrous sound kind | crudeness; a hard sell |
| **Ruth** | plain, warm, unshakeable; speaks of people, not doctrine | argument; theology |

## 7. The event-card grammar

Every event card is built the same way (matches §9 mechanics):

```
TITLE           — a short evocative noun phrase (2-4 words)
[illustration]  — one line for the artist: cross-hatched ink on aged paper (art direction)
SETUP           — 1-3 sentences. One concrete image, one situation, the stakes implied.
CHOICES         — 2-4 options. Each: a stance/imperative + legible cost + a one-line result.
                  (costs render as digits; the ledger's notice is shown, never numbered)
```

**Rules for cards specifically:**
- The **setup** never states the mechanic ("this raises Reckoning"). It shows the world.
- Each **choice** is a *stance* first ("Turn her away."), the cost woven second.
- **Hidden ledger axes (Reckoning, Ghost Roll) are shown only as omen**, never as a number, in consequence prose. Visible axes (coin, food, Morale, Reputation) may name their movement plainly.
- Ignoring a Major card resolves to its worst option (§9) — write that outcome, too.

## 8. Reference cards (the voice at work)

### A — Quiet dread *(Warnings tier, §6)*
**Sour**
*[a scalded tin pail, the milk in it turned grey; a single crow on the rail behind]*
> The morning milk came out of the pail already turned, though the cow is sound and the pail was scalded clean. Josephine won't say it. She only sets it down by the east field, where the ground stays warm through the frost, and goes back to her work.

- **Pour it out and say nothing.** → *You pour it in the yard. The dog won't cross where it soaked in.*
- **Ask Old Nan.** *(if known)* → *She doesn't look up from her mending. "Milk sours where the ground's started listening," she says. "You've laid something down it didn't care for."*
- **It's nothing. Work the day.** → *You work the day. The pail sours again at dusk.*

### B — Opportunity *(the Vane wagon, §3)*
**Vane's Wagon**
*[a painted wagon halted on the dusk road, one lantern lit, a tarp over shapes that might be sleeping]*
> Ambrose Vane's wagon stops at your gate as the light goes. "A Grower," he says, warm as a parlor. "Sharp-minded, strong-backed. I'd not sell her cheap to just anyone — but you've a kind look about you." Under the tarp, something shifts, and is still.

- **Buy her. (110 marks)** → *She steps down when he says her name. She has a name; he tells it to you like a courtesy, and moves straight on to the price.*
- **Haggle.** *(Warm with Ambrose)* → *He smiles, pained and cultured. "For you — a hundred, and you didn't rob me, understand." (100 marks)*
- **Not tonight.** → *"Another season, then. The ground's only getting quicker." He tips his hat, and the wagon rolls on into the dark.*

### C — Town *(the exposure mechanic, §5)*
**The Sheriff's Rounds**
*[a tall man in a dark coat sitting his horse at the fence line, not dismounting, looking past you at the east field]*
> Sheriff Coldwater sits his horse at your line a long while before he speaks. "Heard you're two hands short since spring," he says. He doesn't look at you. He looks at the field. "Ground's soft out here. Things don't always stay buried in it. I'd hate to have to come and look."

- **Invite him to look.** *(nothing to hide)* → *He walks the rows, unhurried, and finds only rows. "Obliged," he says, and means it. (+Reputation)*
- **"They ran off. Hands do."** → *He holds your eye a moment too long. "They do," he agrees, and does not believe you.* (raises exposure risk)
- **"Mind your own line, Sheriff."** → *"It's all my line," he says, and turns his horse. He'll be back. (−Reputation)*

### D — Reckoning *(Walkers tier; a Ghost Roll name in that field, §5/§6)*
**She Comes Back to the Rows**
*[a figure standing among the night barley, faced away, where a field was buried into]*
> You put Marta in the east field in the spring — unmarked, to spare the forty marks and the day of work. Tonight she is standing in it. She is not doing anything. She is only standing where you left her, in the rows, faced away, as though waiting to be told the morning's task.

- **Give her the naming now. (40 marks, a rest day)** → *Grange comes and speaks her name over the ground until dawn. The field is quiet after. It is not forgiveness. It is a debt paid late.*
- **Bar the doors and wait for light.** → *At dawn the barley is trampled flat in a wide ring, and she is gone back down. The household does not speak of it. She will come again.*
- **Go out and speak to her.** *(Warm with Old Nan)* → *[reveals a truth of the Marrow — see #6 reveal-pacing]*

### E — The moral choice *(cruelty vs. yield, §1/§3)*
**The Cotton Won't Wait**
*[a field of cotton burst white under a bruised sky; a clone slumped at the row's end]*
> The cotton is ripe and the paper puts the hail two days out. Elias has been in the rows since first light and is swaying on his feet now. Push him through the night and you'll bring it in whole. Let him rest, and a third of it goes to the storm.

- **Work him through the night.** → *You bring it in whole. Elias doesn't speak the next morning, and neither do the others. The land takes no notice of a hard day — not yet.* (labor now, −Morale)
- **Let him rest. Lose what you lose.** → *A third goes to the hail. Elias mends. The house works easier for a week, and you tell yourself the cotton was only cotton.* (−crop, +Morale)

*Note how E's cruel branch is honest that overwork alone isn't a Reckoning debt — only killing is (§3/§6). The prose must not over-promise the ledger.*

## 9. Dialogue at the four tiers (worked example — Old Nan)

- **Cold:** *"I've nothing for you."* (She does not look up.)
- **Neutral:** *"You want the land read, it'll cost. Not in marks — you'll owe it different."*
- **Warm:** *"Sit. You've the look of someone the ground's started to notice. Tell me what you put down, and where."*
- **Bonded:** *"I'm tired, child. I've kept her quiet a long time — longer than you'd credit. When I'm gone, someone has to hold what I hold. Might be it's you. Might be that's why she let you last this long."*

*(One tier-set like this per NPC belongs in the dialogue work, #13. This one is the reference.)*
